Why a build still matters

A pre-built PC hides the joins. The first time a helpdesk ticket is “no display after a RAM upgrade”, you need to know where each part sits and which cable must click. Building once makes every later repair faster — and stops a £180 board being written off by one missing standoff.

Parts

The parts that will fly in

Click or press Enter/Space to flip a card

CPU

Drops into the socket. Align the gold triangle. Close the load plate — never press the contacts.

Motherboard

The road network. Sits on standoffs. I/O shield first, then the board, then every other part plugs into it.

RAM

DIMM slots — usually 2 and 4 for dual channel. Both end clips must click. Hold the edges only.

M.2 SSD

Slides into the M.2 slot at an angle, then screws down. No SATA cable — it lives on the board.

PSU

Slides into the bottom bay. Switch off while cabling. Never open it — capacitors hold a lethal charge.

GPU

Seats in the long PCIe x16 slot. May need its own 8-pin from the PSU. Monitor cable goes in the GPU, not the board.

Flip two or three as a class. Hold up real parts if you have them. Cooler and I/O shield appear in the fly-in even though they are not on these cards. ~4 min.
Before you buy

Compatibility — the five matches

Socket & chipset

LGA1700 CPU only fits LGA1700. AM5 only fits AM5. The box must agree.

RAM generation

DDR4 will not clip into DDR5 slots. Check the QVL if you push speed.

Form factor

ATX board needs an ATX tray. Mini-ITX fits almost anything; E-ATX may not.

PSU watts & plugs

CPU + GPU + ~150 W headroom. You also need the right 8-pin and PCIe leads.

Cooler height

A 165 mm tower will not close a small case. Check the case spec.

GPU length

A 320 mm card needs clearance past the front fans and PSU shroud.

Walk socket first — it is the most expensive mismatch. The live checker comes after the fly-in. ~3 min.

Why they fly in that order

On the bench (common)

  • CPU into the board on a flat mat
  • RAM clicks while you can see both clips
  • Cooler mounted before the board is in the case
  • Then I/O shield, then board into the tray

What the animation shows

  • I/O shield — you cannot fit it after the board
  • Board on standoffs — every hole needs a foot
  • CPU → RAM → cooler → M.2 in their final homes
  • PSU, GPU, then cables last so nothing snags

Same landing spots

Bench-first is often safer. In-case is fine if you have clear access. Either way, the socket, DIMMs, M.2 screw and 8-pin do not move.

Cables that must click

24-pin ATX

Main board power. The latch faces the clip. If it is not fully home, you get no lights at all.

CPU 8-pin (EPS)

Top-left of the board. The forgotten cable. Fans may twitch; the CPU never really starts.

PCIe GPU power

6-pin or 8-pin into the card — not the board. A 6+2 can split; listen for the click.

SATA data + power

L-shaped data plug. Power is a separate daisy-chain from the PSU. M.2 skips both.

Front panel

PWR SW, HDD LED, RESET. One pin pair each. The manual’s tiny diagram is not optional.

Fan headers

CPU_FAN is required or many boards refuse to POST. Case fans on SYS_FAN or a hub.

Hold up a 24-pin and an 8-pin. Ask which one sits by the CPU socket. ~3 min.

ESD, standoffs and things you never force

Safety

Unplug the PSU. Switch on the back to O. Wrist strap to bare metal. Do not open the PSU. Mind case edges and CPU contacts.

Standoffs = feet

Every motherboard screw hole needs a brass standoff. A screw into a bare tray can short the board on first power.

No force

If a connector will not seat, it is the wrong way round. RAM notches, 24-pin latch, USB 3 header key.

Thermal paste

Pea-size in the centre of the IHS unless the cooler ships pre-pasted. Do not smear with a finger.

Bags until needed

Keep the CPU, RAM and GPU in anti-static bags. Do not stack boards on a wooden bench without a mat.

Demonstrate strap clip. Preview the case study — missing standoff. Cross-link Safe Working & ESD. ~3 min.

First boot — POST then OS

When the Power chip lights the case in the animation, that is POST — firmware counting CPU, RAM and storage before any Windows or Linux logo. No beep / no display is a hardware conversation, not an OS one yet.

1. Picture

Cable in the GPU if you fitted one. Confirm the monitor input.

2. BIOS / UEFI

CPU temp sane, XMP/EXPO if appropriate, boot order = USB installer then NVMe.

3. OS

Install to the SSD you just flew in. Do not leave a leftover HDD as disk 0 by accident.

4. Drivers

Chipset, GPU, LAN. Then Windows Update. Then a 10-minute stress / temp check.

Link BIOS/UEFI and Installing an OS decks. ~3 min.

Case study — Saturday Build Club

Belfast Met February 2025 ATX office PC £180 board written off

Twelve learners built the same study PC. Eleven posted. One went click, smell, dark the moment the front-panel button was pressed. The 24-pin was seated. The PSU switch was on. Fans never moved.

What they found

  • Only four of nine standoffs were fitted
  • One screw went through a hole with no foot
  • The board shorted against the tray
  • The 24-pin area of the PCB was scorched

The five-second habit

  • Count standoffs against the manual diagram
  • Finger-check: no extra brass, no missing brass
  • Board should sit level before any screw
  • Power on only after a second pair of eyes
Named classroom scenario. Ask: “Would a paperclip PSU test have saved this board?” (No — the short is on the tray.) ~4 min.

Evidence checklist

Jargon buster

CPU socket
The keyed hole the processor drops into (e.g. LGA1700, AM5)
Standoff
Brass foot that holds the board off the metal tray so it cannot short
I/O shield
Metal plate that clicks into the case rear before the motherboard
DIMM
Desktop RAM module. DDR4 and DDR5 are different notches — they do not mix
Dual channel
Two matched sticks in the slots the manual paints (often 2 and 4)
Form factor
Physical size standard: ATX, micro-ATX, mini-ITX for boards and cases
24-pin ATX
Main motherboard power connector from the PSU
EPS 8-pin
Dedicated CPU power, usually at the top-left of the board
PCIe x16
Long expansion slot the graphics card seats in
M.2 / NVMe
Stick-style SSD that screws into the board — no SATA data cable
SATA
L-shaped data (and separate power) for 2.5" SSDs and HDDs
POST
Power-On Self-Test — firmware inventory before the OS loads
XMP / EXPO
Profiles that run RAM at the speed on the box, if the board allows it
Front-panel header
Tiny pins for power switch, reset and LEDs — match the manual
ESD
Electrostatic discharge — a shock you may not feel that can kill a chip
Thermal paste
Heat-conductive compound between CPU lid and cooler base
QVL
Qualified Vendor List — RAM kits the board maker has tested
TDP / watt budget
How much heat/power a part draws; PSU must cover CPU + GPU + headroom
Self-paced revision. Filter “8-pin” and “standoff”. ~2 min or homework.
