Core Hardware

The Core Components

Click any card to flip and learn more

CPU

Central Processing Unit — executes billions of instructions per second. Sits in the CPU socket on the motherboard. Never touch the pins underneath!

RAM

Random Access Memory — fast, temporary workspace. Data is lost when power is off. Slots into DIMM slots. Hold by the edges only.

HDD

Hard Disk Drive — spinning magnetic platters for long-term storage. Slower but cheaper per GB. Connects via SATA cable and SATA power.

SSD

Solid State Drive — chip-based storage with no moving parts. Much faster boot and load times. Comes as 2.5" SATA or M.2 NVMe form factor.

GPU

Graphics Processing Unit — renders images, video and 3D graphics. Plugs into the PCIe x16 slot. May need its own power cable from the PSU.

PSU

Power Supply Unit — converts 230V mains AC to 3.3V, 5V and 12V DC for every component. Never open a PSU — capacitors hold lethal charge.

Have learners flip each card. Ask one learner to read the back of CPU aloud. Physically show each part if available. ~5 min.

Input, Output, Storage, Processing

Every piece of IT kit fits one (or more) of these categories:

Input

Keyboard, mouse, scanner, webcam, microphone

Output

Monitor, printer, speakers, GPU video signal

Storage

HDD, SSD, USB stick, M.2 drive, memory card

Processing

CPU, GPU, RAM, motherboard chipset

Tricky ones

A touchscreen is both input and output. A GPU is processing and produces video output. USB ports can be input or output depending what's plugged in.

Quick categorisation drill: call out "webcam" — input. "SSD" — storage. "CPU" — processing. Mention edge cases. ~3 min.
Connectors

Connectors & Cables You Must Recognise

Click to flip — learn what each connector looks like and where it goes

SATA Data

L-shaped 7-pin connector. Plugs HDD/SSD to motherboard SATA port. One cable per drive.

SATA Power

15-pin flat connector from PSU. Powers HDDs, SSDs and some fans. Do not force — keyed to fit one way.

24-pin ATX

Main motherboard power from PSU. Largest connector on the board. Must click firmly until it locks.

8-pin EPS

CPU power connector near the socket. Often 4+4 pin split. High-end CPUs need both 8-pin connectors.

DIMM vs SO-DIMM

Desktop RAM (DIMM) is longer with one offset notch. Laptop RAM (SO-DIMM) is shorter. Notch position prevents wrong insertion.

PCIe Power

6-pin or 8-pin from PSU to graphics card. High-power GPUs may need two connectors. Never use adapters carelessly.

Show real cables if available. Pass around a SATA cable. Emphasise keyed connectors — they only fit one way. ~4 min.

Cooling & Airflow

Cooling Parts

  • CPU heatsink + fan (or AIO liquid cooler)
  • Case fans (intake & exhaust)
  • Thermal paste between CPU and heatsink
  • Fan headers on motherboard (3-pin / 4-pin PWM)
  • GPU has its own onboard fans
HOT AIR OUT COOL AIR IN

Front/bottom = intake · Rear/top = exhaust

Demonstrate airflow with a case fan if available. Mention thermal paste is a one-time application. Overheating = throttling or shutdown. ~3 min.

Power Delivery & the PSU

The PSU label tells you everything: wattage (e.g. 650W), efficiency rating (80 Plus Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum), and rail voltages (+12V, +5V, +3.3V).

Rule of thumb

Add up component wattage + 20% headroom. A mid-range gaming PC needs ~550–650W.

Efficiency matters

80 Plus Gold wastes less energy as heat — quieter and cheaper to run.

Never open a PSU

Capacitors hold lethal charge even when unplugged. If it fails, replace the whole unit.

Modular vs fixed

Modular PSUs let you attach only the cables you need — cleaner builds, better airflow.

Show a PSU label if possible. Stress NEVER open a PSU. Cross-link to Safe Working & ESD deck. ~3 min.

Reading a Spec Sheet

Can you decode this PC specification?

Intel Core i5-12400F · 16GB DDR4-3200 · 512GB NVMe SSD · NVIDIA RTX 3060 · 650W PSU

i5-12400F

Mid-range 6-core CPU. "F" = no built-in graphics — needs a dedicated GPU.

16GB DDR4-3200

16GB RAM, DDR4 type, running at 3200 MHz. Good for gaming and multitasking.

512GB NVMe

Fast M.2 SSD. NVMe is much quicker than SATA. 512GB is moderate capacity.

Walk through each highlighted term. Ask learners to identify the component type for each. This is how they'd read a Currys or Amazon listing. ~4 min.

Anti-Static (ESD) Safe Handling

Why it matters

A static shock you can't even feel (as low as 30V) can silently destroy a CPU, RAM stick or motherboard. Damage is often invisible until the PC won't boot.

Wear a wrist strap

Clip it to bare metal on the case — always, every time

Use an anti-static mat

Work on a grounded, static-safe surface

Hold parts by the edges

Never touch gold pins, contacts or chip surfaces

Store in anti-static bags

Pink or silver bags — keep spares sealed until needed

Demonstrate wrist strap clipping. Full ESD theory is in the Safe Working & ESD deck — mention cross-link. Make strapping up a habit. ~3 min.

Fragility Matrix

Not all parts are equal — know what's rugged and what's delicate

Rugged

  • PC case panels
  • PSU (external only)
  • Case fans
  • Cables
  • Heatsink (no paste)

Handle with Care

  • RAM sticks
  • SSD / HDD
  • GPU
  • Motherboard
  • M.2 drives

Never Touch

  • CPU pins / contacts
  • Capacitors on board
  • Inside a PSU
  • Gold finger contacts
  • Thermal paste surface
Quiz: "Where would you put the CPU?" (Never touch column). "Can you carry a case panel under your arm?" (Rugged). ~2 min.

GPU
Graphics Processing Unit — renders images and video

Power Supply Unit — converts mains AC to DC for components
HDD
Hard Disk Drive — magnetic spinning-platter storage
SSD
Solid State Drive — chip-based storage, no moving parts
Motherboard
Main circuit board connecting all components
PCIe
Peripheral Component Interconnect Express — high-speed expansion slot
SATA
Serial ATA — standard connector for HDDs and SSDs
M.2
Small form-factor slot for NVMe SSDs directly on the motherboard

Desktop and laptop RAM module form factors
ESD
