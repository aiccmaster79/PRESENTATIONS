SharePoint/Teams Setup

Design a usable Microsoft Team, connect it to SharePoint, and set governance before the file chaos begins.

OCN NI Digital Skills · Feature-rich practical deck
Timing: 2 minutes. Welcome learners and frame the session as a practical build: Teams is the front door, SharePoint is the file engine, and governance keeps the space usable.

Learning objectives

  • Explain how a Team, channels, files, and the connected SharePoint site fit together.
  • Build a safe setup sequence: create Team, name channels, assign owners/members, create folders, set sharing defaults, invite users.
  • Choose an appropriate channel and folder structure for class projects or department work.
  • Apply naming, guest access, and lifecycle rules that stop collaboration spaces becoming abandoned storage.
Timing: 3 minutes. Read each objective and ask learners which one they already feel confident about. Use responses to decide how much time to spend on the core concept slides.

Real consequence

A Team created in a hurry can expose files to the wrong audience, hide important resources in chat history, and leave staff unable to archive or hand over ownership later.

Timing: 4 minutes. Ask for examples of messy Teams learners have seen. Keep it constructive: the problem is usually a missing setup process, not careless users.

Teams and SharePoint: the mental model

Teams is the workspace

Chat, meetings, channel posts, tabs, apps, and notifications live where people collaborate day to day.

Channels create context

Channels split work into topics such as Assessment, Projects, Admin, or Year 1 cohort.

SharePoint stores files

The Files tab connects to a SharePoint document library, with folders for each standard channel.

Key idea: do not treat Teams as "just chat". Every setup decision has a file, permission, and lifecycle impact.

Timing: 4 minutes. Draw the front-door/back-room analogy: Teams is the door learners use; SharePoint is where the documents and permissions actually live.

Roles and permissions

Owners

  • Create and delete channels.
  • Manage members, guests, settings, and connectors.
  • Control archive, restore, and handover decisions.

Members and guests

  • Members collaborate inside the Team according to settings.
  • Guests need explicit policy approval and narrower expectations.
  • Visitors may have read-only access in SharePoint when required.

Rule of thumb: at least two active owners, membership via groups where possible, and guest access decided before invitations are sent.

Timing: 5 minutes. Emphasise that ownership is operational resilience. Ask what happens during staff absence if only one owner exists.

    Folders, sharing, invitations

    Channels versus folders

    Use channels for work streams

    • There is a distinct conversation or audience.
    • Notifications and posts need separation.
    • The topic may need tabs, apps, or meetings.

    Use folders for file organisation

    • The audience is the same but documents need grouping.
    • You need templates, drafts, final versions, or evidence folders.
    • The structure should remain understandable in SharePoint.
    Timing: 5 minutes. Explain that overusing channels creates notification noise, while overusing folders can bury files. The goal is a structure that matches how the group works.

    Sharing defaults and guest access

    Default link type

    Prefer organisation-only links for internal collaboration unless a specific external need is approved.

    Expiry and review

    External links and guest memberships should expire or be reviewed on a schedule.

    Sensitive content

    Use private channels, SharePoint permissions, or sensitivity labels when the audience is narrower.

    Before inviting: check whether guests can invite others, whether members can create channels, and what link type appears by default.

    Timing: 5 minutes. This is a risk slide. Ask learners to identify which settings could leak documents accidentally if left at permissive defaults.

    Case study: Northbridge Creative Media

    Before: chaos

    • A lecturer created "Media Stuff" with no naming convention.
    • Only one owner existed; they left mid-year.
    • Students uploaded final evidence, drafts, and feedback into General.
    • Guest freelancers were invited with broad access and no review date.

    After: governed rebuild

    • Renamed to CM-2026-Production-Portfolio.
    • Added programme lead and technician as owners.
    • Created Assessment, Project Groups, Resources, and Staff Planning channels.
    • Set guest expiry, archive review, and starter folder templates.
    Timing: 6 minutes. Tell this as a short story. Ask learners which single change had the greatest effect and why multiple controls worked together.

    Practical task: build a governed Team plan

    Brief

    Create a setup plan for a Level 2 project Team that will hold resources, group work, assessment evidence, and teacher feedback for one term.

    Success criteria

    • Team name follows a convention.
    • At least two owners and membership rules are defined.
    • Channels and folders match the purpose.
    • Sharing defaults and guest policy are stated.
    • Archive or review date is included.

    Evidence checklist

    • Screenshot or sketch of channel list.
    • Folder tree for one important channel.
    • Owner/member/guest table.
    • Welcome post explaining where files go.
    • Short risk note: what could go wrong and how it is controlled.
    Timing: 10 minutes. This can be a paired activity. If software access is unavailable, accept a paper or whiteboard plan as evidence.

    Searchable jargon buster

    15 terms shown.

    Team

    A Microsoft 365 collaboration workspace with members, channels, chats, meetings, and files.

    SharePoint site

    The connected web site that stores files and supports permissions, pages, and lists.

    Channel

    A topic area inside a Team with posts, tabs, and a linked file folder.

    Standard channel

    A channel visible to all Team members.

    Private channel

    A restricted channel for a subset of Team members, with separate membership and file area.

    Shared channel

    A channel that can include people from another organisation when tenant policy allows it.

    Owners

    People who manage Team settings, membership, channels, and lifecycle decisions.

    Members

    People who participate in the Team and usually edit shared files.

    Visitors

    SharePoint users who commonly have read-only access to a site or library.

    Document library

    A SharePoint container for files, folders, versions, metadata, and permissions.

    Folder

    A file grouping inside a library or channel folder.

    Sharing link

    A generated link that controls who can open or edit a file.

    Guest access

    Access for someone outside the organisation, usually with extra policy controls.

    Lifecycle

    The plan for creation, active use, review, archive, retention, and deletion.

    Sensitivity label

    A Microsoft 365 label that can apply protection or classification to a Team, site, or file.

    Timing: 5 minutes. Invite learners to search for terms they have heard but not used confidently. Use this slide as a checkpoint before the quiz.

    Key takeaways

    Purpose first

    A clear purpose drives Team type, channels, membership, and lifecycle.

    Ownership matters

    Two trained owners prevent orphaned spaces and unmanaged guest access.

    Structure should match work

    Use channels for conversations and folders for file organisation.

    Governance is usability

    Naming, sharing defaults, and archive plans make collaboration safer and easier.
