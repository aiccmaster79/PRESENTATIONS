Introducing Strawbees

Straws, connectors and locks — prototype structures you can later bring to life

OCN NI Level 2 Diploma in Information Technology
Welcome. Put a bag of connectors and straws on each table before they sit. Hook: “You already know Lego and VEX — Strawbees is the kit that turns drinking-straw geometry into working machines.” Hold up a 5-leg pink connector. ~2 min.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  • Name the four colour-coded connectors and say what each is for
  • Make a click-lock, a through-lock and a tension lock that actually hold
  • Explain why a triangle stands and a square collapses under load
  • Build a rigid tetrahedron from six equal-length straws
  • Describe how a micro:bit and servo turn a structure into a robot
  • Diagnose a wobbly joint and choose the right lock to fix it
Read the six. Emphasise “actually hold” — lots of first builds look finished but rotate at every vertex. Ask who has used Strawbees, micro:bit, or VEX already so you can pair experienced with new. ~2 min.

Why Strawbees Matters

Engineers do not start in CAD. They start with a cheap prototype that is allowed to fail. Strawbees is that prototype: same straws, same connectors, rebuild in minutes. That “fail fast” loop is the skill employers want — not a perfect tower on the first try.

Discover

What Is a Strawbee?

The name is literal: straw + bee. Each plastic piece is a tiny “bee” with a head (the hole) and one or more legs that clip onto drinking straws. Invented in Gothenburg, Sweden by Erik Thorstensson with Petter Danielson and Oscar Ternbom after a school asked for a kit that would not snap like the expensive one they had bought.

Head

The ring. A leg clicks into it. This is every joint in the kit.

Leg

The spike. Slide it into a straw, or through another head.

Groove

The notch on the leg. Listen for the click when it seats.

Straw

Colour-coded lengths. Same colour = same length. That matters.

Pass around one 1-leg and one 5-leg. Get them to find the groove. “If you do not hear a click, it is not locked.” Mention Sweden / Gothenburg once — quiz later. ~3 min.

Connect

Four Connectors, Four Jobs

Click or press Enter to flip each card

1-leg · Blue

End cap / stopper

Shortest piece. Caps a straw, starts a lock, or pairs with others at a vertex. You will use more of these than you expect — they finish edges and tension locks.

2-leg · Yellow

In-line joint

Two legs. Extends a straw, makes a hinge, or sits in the middle of a long member. Great for moving joints if you only click the groove, not shove it through.

3-leg · Green

Corner / T-joint

The triangle-maker. Three legs meet at a vertex. Default choice for a rigid 2D frame and for most tetrahedron corners.

5-leg · Pink

Hub / complex vertex

The iconic “bee”. Five legs from one head — geodesic hubs, robot mounts, anything that needs many members at one point. Do not force extra legs into a 3-leg problem.

Have each pair flip all four. Ask one learner to summarise 3-leg vs 5-leg. Physical match: hold the real piece next to the colour swatch. ~5 min.

Brace

Why Triangles Win

A square has a secret degree of freedom: it can shear into a diamond without any straw changing length. A triangle cannot — every side locks the other two.

Square / rectangle

  • Looks “finished” until you press the top
  • Joints rotate; the frame becomes a parallelogram
  • Needs a diagonal brace to become two triangles
  • Fine for a moving linkage — disastrous for a tower

Triangle

  • Three members, three vertices — fully braced
  • Load travels down both sides into the base
  • Tetrahedron = four triangles in 3D
  • Default starting shape for any load-bearing build

Technician rule

If it wobbles, count the triangles. If you see a four-sided face with no diagonal, that face is the failure waiting to happen.

Build a live 4-straw square and push the corner — it flops. Add one diagonal. That demo is worth more than the slide. Next slide lets them press Load themselves. ~3 min.

Four Ways to Lock a Joint

Same pieces, different push. The groove decides whether the joint rotates or freezes.

Click-lock

Seat the groove in the head until you hear the click. Holds, but still rotates — good for hinges and first assemblies.

Through-lock

Push the leg all the way through the head. Rotation is limited. Use this when a vertex must not spin.

Tension lock

Leg inside a straw, then a spare head slides along the straw and snaps onto the groove. Stops the straw slipping off.

Friction lock

Fold a head over and snap it to the tip of the leg. The exposed legs can still shift, but they hold a pose.

First-build mistake

Pushing too softly looks assembled and fails under load. If in doubt: unclick, listen for the click again, then tension-lock the vertex that still spins.

Live demo each lock on the visualiser. Pass one tension-lock around. Tell them the practical will require at least one tension lock on the tower. ~4 min.

From Structure to Robot

Today is structures. The same joints later carry a brain.

micro:bit clip

Yellow clip + black connector board. The micro:bit slides in; the clip snaps onto a Strawbees vertex.

Servo + mount

A servo is a motor that turns to an angle you code. Mount it so the horn pushes a linkage — grabbers, wings, walkers.

MakeCode extension

Add Strawbees Robotic Inventions in Microsoft MakeCode. Blocks drive servos from buttons, tilt, or pins.

Structure first

A floppy arm wastes a perfect program. Brace the frame, then add the servo. Code cannot fix a square that shears.

Case study

Utica Community Schools

In Michigan, teacher Chris trialled Strawbees with a micro:bit extension he already had from DonorsChoose. The trial stuck. Instructional technology coach Cory Widener then rolled Strawbees STEAM Classroom kits across all 25 elementary schools in Utica Community Schools — a district-wide engineering kit, not a one-off club toy.

25Elementary schools
64Mini-pyramids → one big pyramid
2Hardest first skills: matching connectors & the elbow

What the trial taught

Young builders struggled to pair the right connectors and to use the elbow joint. After practice they built huge pyramids from 64 smaller ones. Scale comes from repeating a rigid unit — the same tetra / triangle you will build today — not from inventing a new shape every time.

Named people and a real district — not a hypothetical. Teaching point: mastery of two joints unlocked a giant structure. Our tower challenge uses the same idea. Source: Strawbees Utica case study. ~4 min.

Common Faults

SymptomLikely causeFix
Joint spins under a light push Groove only half-seated, or click-lock used where you needed a through-lock Unclick, listen for the click, then push the leg through the head
Straw slides off a connector No tension lock; straw not pushed onto the groove Slide a spare head along the straw and snap it onto the groove
“Square” tower leans Mixed straw lengths on one face, or no diagonal brace Rebuild the face with one colour; add a diagonal to make two triangles
Vertex will not take a third straw Wrong connector (1-leg or 2-leg at a 3-way corner) Swap in a 3-leg (green) or 5-leg (pink) hub
Servo twitches, arm barely moves Floppy structure eating the torque, or horn not on a linkage Brace the mount with triangles; check the servo screw and MakeCode pin
Pieces “lost” at tidy-up 1-leg blues hiding inside straws; pink hubs in pockets Count back into colour trays before anyone leaves — blues first
Use this as the helpdesk script during the practical. Point at the leaning-tower row before they start building. ~3 min.

Practical: Load-Bearing Tower

Brief (pairs, ~20 min build): Design and build a free-standing Strawbees tower at least 30 cm tall that holds a 500 ml water bottle for 10 seconds without hand support.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two triangles (faces or braces)
  • Includes at least one tension lock
  • Each face uses one straw length
  • Stands alone and holds the bottle 10s
  • Can be fully disassembled back into trays

Evidence checklist

  • Front + side photo of the loaded tower
  • Sketch with three joints labelled (click / through / tension)
  • 30-second demo: place bottle, count to ten
  • One sentence: what failed first, and the fix
  • Piece count returned to the colour trays
Read the brief. Ban mixing lengths on one face. Water bottles stay capped. If a pair finishes early, add a 10 cm height bonus or a moving joint at the top. Photograph evidence before tidy-up. ~5 min briefing; 20 min build after the quiz or before it if you prefer build-then-debrief.

Jargon Buster

Strawbee
A plastic connector with a head (hole) and one or more legs that clip to straws
Head
The ring on a connector that a leg clicks into
Leg
The spike that goes into a straw or through another head
Groove
Notch on the leg that seats in a head — listen for the click
Click-lock
Groove seated in the head; holds but still rotates
Through-lock
Leg pushed fully through the head so rotation is limited
Tension lock
Spare head snapped onto a grooved leg inside a straw to stop slip
Friction lock
Head folded over and snapped to the leg tip to hold a pose
Vertex
A corner where two or more members meet
Member
One straw acting as a structural beam or link
Triangle / brace
Three-member loop that cannot shear; the default rigid unit
Tetrahedron
Four triangular faces, six edges, four vertices — a 3D rigid unit
Shear
A square flattening into a diamond without straws changing length
Servo
A motor that turns to a coded angle; drives grabbers and walkers
micro:bit clip
Mount that holds a micro:bit on a Strawbees structure
MakeCode extension
Strawbees Robotic Inventions blocks that drive servos from the micro:bit
Prototype
A cheap model you expect to change — Strawbees’ whole point
Hub
A many-legged connector (usually 5-leg pink) where lots of members meet

Q1: The pink Strawbees connector has how many legs?

A3
B5
C4
D2
Answer: B — Pink is the 5-leg hub. Green is 3-leg, yellow 2-leg, blue 1-leg. There is no 4-leg in the standard colour set.
Easy colour check. If they say 4, they are mixing Lego thinking. ~1 min.

Q2: Why does a triangle stay standing when you push the top, but a square does not?

ATriangles use stronger plastic
BSquares always have shorter straws
CA square can shear into a diamond without changing straw length
DTriangles have more connectors, so they must be stiffer
Answer: C — Geometry, not material. Add a diagonal and the square becomes two triangles.
The load-test widget is the hint. ~1 min.

Q3: You push a connector leg all the way through another connector’s head. What have you made?

AA click-lock that still rotates freely
BA through-lock with limited rotation
CA tension lock on the straw
DA servo mount
Answer: B — Through the head = freeze the joint. Tension lock is the extra head on a straw.
Common mix-up with tension lock. ~1 min.

Q4: Strawbees was invented in which city?

ABelfast
BGothenburg, Sweden
CTokyo
DDetroit
Answer: B — Erik Thorstensson, Petter Danielson and Oscar Ternbom in Gothenburg. Utica (Michigan) is the school case study, not the invention.
Detroit is a trap from the Utica story. ~1 min.

Q5: The Strawbees robotic kit is designed to clip which board onto a structure?

AArduino Uno
BRaspberry Pi 5
Cmicro:bit
DVEX Brain
Answer: C — Yellow clip + connector board for the micro:bit. VEX is a different kit in this library (#141).
VEX trap is deliberate — same room, different ecosystem. ~1 min.

Q6: A tower leans and one straw is creeping off its connector. What should you try first?

AAdd a servo to hold it
BTension-lock that straw, then brace any four-sided face with a diagonal
CCut the straw shorter so it grips
DSwap every connector for a 5-leg pink
Answer: B — Slip = tension lock. Lean = missing triangle. Cutting classroom straws breaks the colour-length system.
Fault-table question. Cutting straws is the forbidden “fix”. ~1 min.

Key Takeaways

Colour is a spec

Blue 1 · yellow 2 · green 3 · pink 5. Same straw colour = same length.

Click, then lock

Hear the groove. Through-lock to freeze. Tension-lock to stop slip.

Triangles carry load

If a face has four sides, add a diagonal or expect shear.

Prototype, then robot

Rigid unit first. micro:bit and servo only after the frame stands.
