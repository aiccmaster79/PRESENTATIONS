The hook

Call both of them “the robot” and the next demo is already a fault.

SRC has two physical units. They do not share a battery, a log, or a bruise.

If a hall walk goes wrong, the technician who mixed the serials cannot say which policy, which pack, or which last service applied. If you also treat a 1.32 m education humanoid like a film stunt or a factory cell, the likely outcome is a fall into a person — not a viral clip.

Objectives

By the end of this explainer you will be able to:

  • Identify SRC’s robots as Unitree G1 EDU humanoids — not Atlas, not the taller H1, not a VEX Base Bot in vinyl. Emerging technology — name the kit accurately
  • Contrast this programmable humanoid with factory automation that repeats one job in a cage. Robotics vs automation
  • State hall-ready skills (walk, kick, sit, map) and hard limits (payload, battery, unsupervised people). Responsible use of kit
  • Sequence how a gait is trained: simulate, reward, randomise, then sim-to-real on the metal. Emerging technology — how the skill arrived
Core concept

Four rungs. Hang every photo on this ladder.

A humanoid on Open Day is not a mascot costume. It is a platform: sensors in, motors out, a trained policy in the middle, and a human still owning the floor.

01 · Name it

Four labels on the same machine

Unitree

The manufacturer. Known for quadruped “robot dogs” and compact humanoids. SRC bought the education humanoid line, not a toy.

G1

The compact model: about 132 cm, about 35 kg, two legs. Walks, kicks, sits, folds for a crate. Not the ~180 cm H1.

EDU

Education pack: extra degrees of freedom, ~3 kg arm payload, NVIDIA Jetson Orin, SDK (Python, C++, ROS 2). The show-only Standard does not give you that kit.

SRC

Southern Regional College. Orange, blue and purple vinyls on chest and thighs. Two units — treat them as two assets.

02 · Body

What has to work for a kick to land

  • 1 Head — depth camera and 3D LiDAR map the room.
  • 2 Chest — SRC asset mark; inside, compute and battery path.
  • 3 Hands — five-finger (dexterous) grip, not a stump.
  • 4 Knees — EDU actuators, about 120 N·m. Strong, not a forklift.
  • 5 Feet — the whole pose is a live balance problem.
See
Depth camera plus Livox-class 3D LiDAR.
Think
8-core CPU plus Jetson Orin on EDU for on-board AI.
Talk
Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.2, four mics, a speaker.
Power
9,000 mAh quick-swap pack — about two hours, then a ~30 s swap.
DoF
About 23–43 independent joints, depending on wrists and hands.
Move
Walk about 2 m/s on the flat; sit; fold to roughly 69 cm.

This is robotics: sense, decide, act in a human building. It is not a caged arm that only paints the same door all shift (automation).

03 · Train

A gait is a policy, not a typed routine

Nobody ships walk_nicely() for every joint. The G1 runs a policy: if I sense this, move the motors like that.

1 Demo

A human teleoperates or records a motion.

→
2 Simulate

Thousands of falls on a GPU, not on parquet.

→
3 Reward

Stay up and move forward scores. Fall does not.

→
4 Randomise

Friction, mass, light — so it is not only good in the fake world.

→
5 Sim-to-real

Same policy on the metal G1. A human still spots.

Imitation learning copies a demonstration. Reinforcement learning searches by score. EDU’s SDK is how SRC students will eventually talk to the robot; the hall photos are already the transfer step working in a real building.

Trade-offs

What the spec sheet will not shout

Bipedal vs wheels

Two legs fit doors and stairs made for people. They also fall. A wheeled trolley would not kick — and would not need the same exclusion zone.

EDU vs Standard

EDU is why you can write Python / ROS 2. A show-only unit is a demo appliance. If the SDK will not connect, first ask which pack you actually have.

3 kg, not a person

Arm payload is a tool or a bottle. Asking it to carry a classmate is a use-of-kit failure, not a stretch goal.

Robot vs automation

A paint-shop arm repeats a known path behind a fence. The G1 is sold as a general platform in human spaces. That is more interesting — and more of a duty of care.

~2 hours, then swap

Open Day is not an all-day unsupervised shift. Plan packs the way you plan extension leads.

Two names or mixed logs

Identical vinyls. Different serials. If both are “the robot,” fault history is fiction.

Common faults

Symptom → likely cause → fix

Symptom Likely cause Fix
Won’t stand / limp joints E-stop, flat pack, or motors not enabled Find the emergency stop, swap the 9,000 mAh pack, follow EDU power-up order
Falls mid-demo Uneven floor, crowd in the policy, or a trick that only worked in sim Clear the floor, spotter in reach, do not improvise new skills live
SDK will not connect Show-only Standard, or Wi-Fi / power off Confirm EDU pack, same network, robot powered, Unitree / ROS 2 docs
Motors hot / shutdown Long high-torque sequence without rest Pause between routines; air cooling is not magic

Jargon

G1 EDU
Unitree’s compact education humanoid (~132 cm). EDU adds SDK access, extra joints, payload and an on-board AI module.
H1
Unitree’s taller adult-size humanoid (~180 cm). SRC’s photos are G1-scale, not H1.
Degree of freedom (DoF)
One independent joint motion. More DoF means more poses — and more things to control.
LiDAR
A laser scanner that builds a 3D point cloud so the robot knows where walls are.
Actuator
Motor-plus-gearbox at a joint. EDU knees can push about 120 N·m.
Payload
Mass an arm can hold usefully. About 3 kg on G1 EDU.
Policy
The trained “if I sense this, move like that” function from learning.
Reinforcement learning
Trial and error with a score. Stay upright and move forward = points.
Imitation learning
Copy a human demonstration instead of discovering the skill from scratch.
Teleoperation
A person drives the robot live. Useful for demos and for recording data.
Sim-to-real
Train in a physics simulator, then run the same policy on the metal robot.
SDK / ROS 2
EDU software kit. Students can write Python or C++; ROS 2 is common robotics middleware.
E-stop
Emergency stop. First thing you locate before a demo.
Bipedal
Walks on two legs. Easy to fall compared with four wheels or four legs.
Automation (here)
A machine repeating a known job, often in a fixed cell. Contrast with a general humanoid platform.
