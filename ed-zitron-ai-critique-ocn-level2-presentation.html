#148·Level 2·Emerging technology

Calling BS on AI

Ed Zitron on The Diary Of A CEO — follow the money, test the myths, and judge a 2027 forecast like a technician, not a fan.

OCN NI Level 2 — Understanding Emerging Technology (A/651/0447)

Source: youtube.com/watch?v=Lf5oqGOCRCM

Timing: 2 min. Cue: this is a media-literacy lesson, not an anti-AI rant. Name the source, the guest, and the job of the hour — evaluate claims. Open with: we already use chatbots; this hour is about whether the industry story matches the books.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  • State Ed Zitron’s core claim: generative AI is sold as magic, but billed as expensive, unprofitable cloud software.
  • Contrast three industry myths (growth, jobs, “the race”) with the evidence he offers.
  • Explain capex, circular deals, and why a 2027 cash crunch is his forecast — not a fact.
  • Evaluate Steven Bartlett’s counter: early tech often looks worse and unprofitable before it wins.
  • Apply a four-question check before you trust an AI headline in an assignment.
Timing: 3 min. Cue: read the verbs — state, contrast, explain, evaluate, apply. This deck has no quiz widgets; the “test” is whether they can argue both sides without slogans.

The source

Guest — Ed Zitron

  • ~16 years in tech PR; writes the newsletter Where’s Your Ed At
  • Loves technology; hates being sold “magic”
  • Thesis: the pitch overstates capability, timeline, and profit
  • Forecast: OpenAI runs out of steam — and cash — around 2027

Host — Steven Bartlett

  • The Diary Of A CEO (long-form interview)
  • Pushes back with adoption, the innovator’s dilemma, and cheaper-over-time history
  • Horse → car analogy: early cars broke down and still won
  • Closes by thanking a rare contrarian voice

Video: “The Man Who Calls BS On AI … 2027 Is When It All Breaks! | Ed Zitron” — The Diary Of A CEO.

Timing: 3 min. Cue: two people, two jobs. Zitron prosecutes the pitch. Bartlett stress-tests it. Learners should be able to name both without collapsing them into “the video says”.

Why this matters

Your course already uses AI

Chatbots, prompts, and “AI will take jobs” sit in #116, #124 and #128. This hour is the sceptical twin.

Headlines are a product

If every outlet repeats “this will take your job,” adoption is not the same as honest demand.

IT people follow invoices

A technician asks: who pays, who profits, what fails, and what happens if the money stops.

Consequence

If you write “AI is inevitable” in an assessment with no cost, power, or reliability line, you have described marketing — not emerging technology.

Timing: 3 min. Cue: link to Green IT (#129) and Robots & Automation (#128). The consequence line is the hook — assessments need cost and risk, not slogans.

The core claim

“I think generative AI is at its heart a con … they’ve sold it in the terms of magic … it’s boring cloud software that’s extremely expensive and unprofitable and also unreliable at its core.”

Ed Zitron, paraphrased from the interview opening

Sold as

Replace all jobs. Cure cancer. Autonomous. Set and forget. AGI is coming.

Runs as

A large language model in a data centre: prompts, tokens, GPUs, electricity, human cleanup.

His test

If it were honest software, vendors would publish AI revenue, a path to profit, and failure rates on real work.

Timing: 4 min. Cue: “con” is his word — learners may use “over-sold” in coursework. Unpack LLM vs AGI. Stress unreliable-at-core: hallucinations are not a UI bug in his view.

Three myths he knocks down

Bartlett plays a myth game. Zitron’s answers, in one line each:

Claim you will hear Zitron’s reply What to check
The AI industry is creating enormous economic growth The labs run at a “horrifying loss.” He cites OpenAI losing $20.9 billion in one year. Profit vs revenue. Who is still being subsidised?
AI will replace all human jobs “That just isn’t happening and there’s no economic data to support it.” Unemployment stats, not demo videos.
The US must spend trillions to beat China What is the race for? “Wrong hands” already include the people shipping the models. National-security language vs a product pitch.
Timing: 4 min. Cue: do not let the class treat “no jobs lost yet” as “no jobs will change.” The claim he rejects is total replacement with no data.

Capex versus what comes back

Capex = long-term spend (data centres, GPUs). OpEx = running costs (power, staff). His complaint: spend is in the trillions; AI sales are not.

>$1TCapex already spent, he says — another trillion wanted next year
70%Of “AI revenue” at the big clouds, he says, is OpenAI + Anthropic
$10BMicrosoft AI leftover after OpenAI, vs $115B capex (his FY2026 read)

Microsoft snapshot (as he tells it): ~$34.33B related take, $24.1B of that from OpenAI, ~$10B left — in a year with $115B capex and $175B planned. “The math does not make sense.”

Also cited: Nvidia ~$215.9B of GPU sales supporting ~$22B of AI revenue outside the two unprofitable labs.

Timing: 5 min. Cue: draw capex vs revenue on a board. Stress these are his Bloomberg-style reads. A Level 2 answer can say “spend dwarfs disclosed AI profit” without memorising every dollar.

Circular money

Progress, he says, only continues while cash is circulated into the two labs that cannot yet stand alone.

Cloud giants

Amazon, Google, Microsoft book “AI growth” from labs they also fund.

→

The labs

OpenAI and Anthropic: huge users of GPUs and cloud, still unprofitable.

→

Chip vendor

Nvidia sells the GPUs — and, he argues, helps keep the buyers alive.

Cheques he names

Amazon → OpenAI $50B; Amazon → Anthropic $5B; Google → Anthropic $10B. Recent OpenAI funding mix: SoftBank, Nvidia, Amazon. Sell-side hopes: $400B+ of lab revenue in ~3.5 years that has to come from somewhere.

Timing: 4 min. Cue: circular is not automatically fraud — but it is not the same as millions of customers paying cost-plus. Ask: if OpenAI paused spending, whose “AI revenue” falls?

“The largest non-consensual push”

Bartlett’s push

  • Fastest product adoption in tech history
  • Hundreds of millions of people choose to use it
  • Money is a lagging indicator — invest ahead of monetisation

Zitron’s reply

  • Gemini in Google / Docs, Copilot in Word, Rufus on Amazon — the button is in your face
  • Three years of “this will take your job if you don’t”
  • Most use is search-shaped because search got worse, not because the model is a colleague

When Uber burned an annual token budget in three months, he says, “the moment people actually had to pay for it” the romance cooled.

Timing: 4 min. Cue: ask the class — did you turn Copilot on, or did it appear? That is the difference between demand and distribution.

What the product actually is

Ask an “AI person” for their setup and you get a Rube Goldberg of prompts and model-switching — “Pee-wee’s Playhouse.” That is not set-and-forget intelligence.

LLM

Predicts likely next tokens from training data. Useful. Not a mind. Not AGI.

Human in the loop

Right prompt, right model, review the output. The “autonomous agent” still needs a babysitter.

Vibe-coding risk

If you have not written the code yourself for months, you may not spot the security hole the model left open.

Timing: 4 min. Cue: connect to #124 AI in Your Pocket. Good prompting is a skill because the product is not autonomous. AGI/superintelligence = theory, not a shipping spec.

Hallucinations are not a cute bug

Host’s lived experience

  • ChatGPT used to invent facts more often
  • Now: weaker reasoning sometimes, fewer outright hallucinations
  • Simple-task leaderboards: ~21.8% → ~0.7% error on summarisation (figures he quotes)

Zitron’s lived experience

  • Bloomberg “ask B” invented a Microsoft share price that never existed
  • Cute on a newsletter; dangerous in a clinic or a hedge-fund model
  • “Agent” demos that run for an hour at ~50% success are not a junior engineer

Multiplicative failure

One wrong line in a refactor can open a door. If you have been vibe-coding, you may have forgotten how to look. He does not believe you can train that fully out.

Timing: 4 min. Cue: both can be true — simple tasks got cleaner; high-stakes tasks still need a human owner. Software quality at GitHub/Meta is his “are products actually better?” test.

Horse, car, or a very expensive horse?

The Innovator’s Dilemma is Bartlett’s frame. Early cars broke down, cost too much, and needed a red-flag walker. They still ate the horse.

If the analogy holds

5% better, 5% cheaper, month after month, and the ceiling is higher than any human coder. Hallucinations become as rare as a stall.

Why he rejects it

Moore’s law is not a GPU law. The whole planet already threw talent, governments, and credit at this. “Shipped tokens” is not “better software.” Cars did not have every scientist on Earth and a media megaphone from day one.

Scale check he uses vs other “unprofitable for years” stories: Spotify did not lose $20.9B in one year; Uber’s lifetime losses and early AWS capex are a different order of magnitude.

Timing: 5 min. Cue: this is the intellectual heart of the hour. Let two learners steelman each side. Do not pick a winner for them.

Power, chips, debt

Worked example from the interview: Stargate Abilene — OpenAI + Oracle, Texas.

1.2 GWCampus power he cites — eight buildings
50kNvidia GB200 GPUs per building (his figure)
DebtAll but Microsoft, he says, are borrowing to keep building

He compresses a city’s worth of electricity into a tiny footprint. GPUs are specialised: if the AI bet fails, you do not simply turn the hall into a “normal” cloud. That is the Green IT overlap — energy is not a rounding error.

Timing: 3 min. Cue: the Bristol comparison in the auto-caption mixed units — do not copy it. Keep 1.2 GW and “specialised GPUs are not general-purpose leftovers.”

2027 — when it “all breaks”?

The title is a forecast. Teach it as a sequence, not a prophecy.

1. Labs need more cash

Better models, in his view, only come from tens of billions more training spend.

→

2. Raise gets hard

OpenAI IPO slipped; CFO talk of “2027 or earlier.” Anthropic might list first.

→

3. Cash runs short

Nvidia or private credit (Blackstone / BlackRock-style) may patch — then not.

→

4. Clouds restate

Microsoft, Google, Amazon cut AI growth guidance. He calls that a “tech depression.”

WeWork-shaped warning

If they “bum-rush” a listing with worse economics than Anthropic, public markets can be brutal. SoftBank sits on a huge paper bet. Many ways to fail; the shared risk is OpenAI as a single point of demand.

Timing: 5 min. Cue: write 2027 on the board with a question mark. Learners must say “Zitron predicts” not “AI dies in 2027.”

Who gets hit if the bet fails

Index funds

Retail money in the “Magnificent 7.” He flags Nvidia as a large slice of the S&P 500 story.

Nvidia

Circular financing: fund the customer so they can borrow to buy more GPUs. He sketches 50–70% revenue down if demand cracks.

Everyone else

Private credit in data centres; Japanese SoftBank exposure; governments that treated the spend as industrial policy.

His deeper theory: big tech is out of hypergrowth ideas. Buying GPUs lets them say “we’re working on AI — don’t think too hard” while ads and search only have so many tweaks left.

Timing: 3 min. Cue: this is systems thinking for Level 2 — one company’s cash problem can be a market problem if everyone is the customer.

Jobs, AGI, and “it went rogue”

Jobs

Tools change tasks. Total replacement needs labour-market evidence. Demo clips are not a census.

AGI / consciousness

“Anyone saying this stuff will become this is just guessing and does not have proof.” Treat as science fiction until it ships.

Blackmail / escape stories

He reads system-card drama as sandbox mis-setup plus media hunger — not proof the model has a will.

“Wrong hands”

If the fear is “what if the model falls into the wrong hands,” his punchline is that it is already in the hands of Zuckerberg, Altman, and Amodei — people he does not trust with the pitch.

Timing: 3 min. Cue: keep it Level 2 — we do not need a philosophy of mind. We need: no proof, so do not write it as fact.

Four questions before you trust a claim

  • Who is selling it? Founder, chip vendor, cloud, journalist, or a critic with a newsletter? Incentives differ.
  • What is the unit? Users, tokens, “run rate,” profit, or completed jobs? Run rate, he says, is undefined on purpose.
  • What happens when it is wrong? Newsletter typo vs clinical note vs production code.
  • What if the subsidy stops? Would this product still exist at today’s quality if OpenAI could not raise?
Timing: 4 min. Cue: this is the transferable skill. They can use it on any emerging-tech article, not just this guest.

Common faults — talking about AI

Symptom in an assignment Likely cause Fix
“AI will take all jobs / cure cancer.” Copied the pitch Write what the tool does today, plus a limit
“Everyone uses it, so it must be profitable.” Confusing users with profit Ask who subsidises inference
“The model is autonomous.” Marketing language Describe prompt → check → edit
“Hallucinations are basically gone.” Only tested easy tasks Name a high-stakes fail mode
“AI dies in 2027.” Treating a forecast as a fact Attribute: Zitron predicts a cash crunch
One source, no numbers Media literacy miss Second source: filing, Ofcom, or a rival guest

Jargon buster

TermPlain English in this lesson
Generative AI / LLMSoftware that produces text, code or images by predicting likely tokens.
AGIHypothetical general intelligence. Not a current product spec.
CapexCapital expenditure — buildings and chips you hope last years.
InferenceRunning a trained model to answer a user. Costs money every time.
Run rateA guessed yearly total from a short window. Easy to massage.
Circular dealA funds B so B can buy A’s product; both report “growth.”
HallucinationFluent output that is false. Still a risk on hard tasks.
IPO / going publicListing on a stock market so early investors can sell.
Private creditBig lenders (not high-street banks) funding data centres.
CUDANvidia’s software layer that made GPUs usable for this wave of AI.
Timing: 3 min. Cue: pick three terms to hear back from the room — capex, circular, hallucination.

Practical task

Watch 20 minutes, then write a one-page brief

Source: The Diary Of A CEO — Ed Zitron. Pause on any stat you intend to quote and mark it claimed.

  • Success criteria: name both speakers; state the core claim; give one myth with his reply; give one Bartlett counter; give one 2027 step; end with your verdict and why.
  • Evidence: timestamp or quote; a second source for any dollar figure; a sentence on power or reliability.
  • Out of scope: “AI is fake” or “AI is god.” Neither is a Level 2 answer.
Timing: 4 min. Cue: this can be homework. Collect briefs next session. Grade the evaluation, not which side they pick.

Key takeaways

Pitch ≠ product

Magic language hides token-billed cloud software that still needs a human checker.

Follow the subsidy

Users and “run rate” are not profit. Circular cheques can look like demand.

2027 is a forecast

His sequence: raise fails, cash thins, clouds restate. Attribute it. Do not treat it as destiny.

Keep both brains

Bartlett’s early-car argument is real. So is Zitron’s “show me the books.” Good IT people use both.

Timing: 3 min. Cue: four pillars on fingers — pitch, subsidy, forecast, both-brains.
