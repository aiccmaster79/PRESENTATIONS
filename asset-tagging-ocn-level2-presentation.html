Why this matters

No tag, no trail

Without a unique asset ID and an up-to-date register, lost devices become expensive write-offs. In schools and workplaces, missing kit can also raise safeguarding and data-protection concerns.

Real consequence: Three untagged laptops disappear from a trolley. Nobody can prove who last signed them out, warranty claims stall, and finance must write them off.

RegisterOwner & location

Why tags matter

Theft & loss

Visible IDs deter casual theft and help recover or report missing kit quickly.

Warranty

Serial and purchase records speed repairs, RMA and insurance claims.

Audit

Finance and inspectors expect physical kit to match the asset register.

Refresh planning

Age and status fields show which devices need replacing next budget year.

Timing: 4 minutes. Ask which reason matters most to a head of IT versus a finance officer.

Label fields that belong on kit

Must show

  • Asset ID (unique tag)
  • Serial number
  • Owner / assigned user
  • Location / room
  • Status (in use, spare…)

Often useful

  • Organisation name
  • Purchase / warranty date
  • Model / type
  • QR or barcode link
  • Support contact code

Never print

  • Passwords or PIN
  • Wi-Fi keys
  • Personal data beyond job role
  • Admin credentials
  • Full home address
Timing: 5 minutes. Stress that labels are public-facing. Secrets belong in a secure system, not on the chassis.

The asset register

One source of truth

The register (spreadsheet, CMDB or asset tool) holds the live record for every tagged device. The physical label and the digital row must agree.

  • Update on assign, transfer, repair and disposal.
  • Use controlled status values, not free-text chaos.
  • Keep serials exact — mistyped serials break warranty work.
StatusMeaning
In stockTagged, ready to assign
AssignedWith a named owner / room
In repairTemporarily out of service
RetiredEnd of life, awaiting dispose
DisposedWiped / recycled, closed record
Timing: 5 minutes. Show how a stale "Assigned" status is worse than no record — it creates false confidence.

Assign, transfer and retire

Assign

  • Record owner and location
  • Issue with imaging / accounts ready
  • User acknowledges receipt if required

Transfer

  • Update register before kit moves
  • Clear old location and owner
  • Re-image or reset permissions if needed

Retire

  • Mark end of support or refresh cycle
  • Remove from active loan pools
  • Queue for secure wipe and dispose
Technician rule: Never hand a device to a new person without updating the register. A transfer without a record is how audits fail.
Timing: 5 minutes. Link transfer to Account Permissions and Imaging decks — moving kit often needs identity and image changes too.

Audit spot-checks

What you actually do

  • Pick a sample of tags from the register.
  • Find the physical device and read the label.
  • Confirm owner, location and status match.
  • Log mismatches and escalate missing kit.

Sample, don't guess

Random or risk-based samples beat checking only the tidy cupboard.

Year-end pressure

Finance audits often coincide with trolley counts and loan returns.

Timing: 4 minutes. Emphasise evidence: photo of tag, register export, signed discrepancy log.

Case study: missing trolley laptops

3Devices missing
£0 tagsNo physical IDs
StaleRegister not updated
Write-off+ safeguarding risk

What happened

  • Year-end audit of laptop trolley
  • Three machines unaccounted for
  • Some kits never tagged on receipt

Why it failed

  • No asset IDs on chassis
  • Loan sheet vs register out of sync
  • Transfers between rooms not logged

Fixes

  • Tag on receive before issue
  • Mandatory transfer updates
  • Termly spot-checks + wipe policy
Timing: 7 minutes. Discuss safeguarding: student data on lost laptops, insurance conditions, and who gets informed.

Jargon buster

Asset tagA unique physical identifier stuck or etched on equipment and linked to the register.
Asset registerThe official list of devices with IDs, serials, owners, locations and status.
Serial numberManufacturer unique ID used for warranty, support and identification.
ProcureThe purchase stage: ordering and approving the device before it arrives.
ReceiveChecking delivery against the order, condition and serials before tagging.
AssignGiving a tagged device to a person, room or role and recording it.
TransferChanging owner or location and updating the register at the same time.
Audit spot-checkSampling physical kit against register rows to find mismatches.
RetireMarking a device as end of life and removing it from active use.
Secure disposeWiping data, recycling or destroying kit, then closing the register record.
Warranty / RMAManufacturer repair or return process that needs accurate serial and purchase data.
CMDBConfiguration Management Database — a structured inventory used in larger IT teams.
Refresh cycleThe planned schedule for replacing ageing devices, often tied to budgets.
Safeguarding concernRisk that lost school devices may hold student or staff personal data.
Timing: 6 minutes. Give two minutes to search three terms, then ask learners to use one term in a sentence about the case study.

Key takeaways

Follow the trail

Procure → receive → tag → assign → transfer → audit → retire → dispose.

Core label fields

Asset ID, serial, owner, location and status — never passwords.

Audit the truth

Spot-checks compare physical claims with the register and log mismatches.

Connect the service

Tagging sits with catalogue, SLA, imaging, permissions and backup.
