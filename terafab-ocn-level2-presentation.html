Terafab

A terawatt chip factory — scale, silicon, and why the world is short of compute

OCN NI Level 2 Diploma in Information Technology
Hook with the night render behind you: Giga Texas is the tiny Tesla rectangle. Terafab is the rest of the picture. Ask “How big would a factory have to be to make a trillion watts of AI chips a year?” Pause. Then start. ~2 min.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  • Explain what Terafab is, who is building it, and why the name starts with tera-
  • Sequence a chip through design, mask, wafer, package and test in one building
  • Sort chips and uses into Earth (edge inference) versus space (orbital compute)
  • Judge the scale, water, power and community trade-offs on the Grimes County site
Verb-led objectives. Write “tera = trillion” on the board now — learners mix it up with terabyte all lesson otherwise. ~3 min.

Why This Matters

Every phone, car computer, robot and AI model runs on chips. Foundries such as TSMC, Samsung and Intel already run near capacity. If Tesla, SpaceX and xAI cannot buy enough silicon, Optimus robots, Cybercabs and orbital data centres simply do not ship.

1 TWAI compute target per year
~2%of that demand today’s fabs can cover
80%of Terafab output aimed at space

The consequence

Musk’s line at the launch: “We either build the Terafab, or we don’t have the chips.” That is a supply-chain sentence, not a slogan. Software cannot invent wafers.

Keep it Level 2: chips are physical objects made in factories. Software people forget that. Contrast with the AI-in-your-pocket deck — prompting is cheap; silicon is not. ~4 min.

Headline Numbers

100 million+ sq ft

Planned manufacturing floor. One of the largest factory footprints ever drawn — the night render puts Giga Texas in the gap between the two wings.

1 terawatt / year

Not a terabyte. A trillion watts of AI compute capacity as the annual output target — the “tera” in Terafab.

Two Texas sites

Prototype beside Giga Texas in Austin. Full-scale plant at Gibbons Creek Reservoir, Grimes County, northwest of Houston.

Published cost figures range from about $17 billion (first cheques) to $55–119 billion (all phases). Treat every dollar figure as reported, not a purchase order you can audit in class.

Write tera vs giga vs mega on the board. Do not get lost in capex arguments. The teaching point is scale plus two-site plan. ~4 min.

Why the loop matters

If a test fails on Tuesday, the mask shop is down the corridor. That is the Giga Texas prototype’s job: a few thousand wafers a month to try ideas — not 100,000 wafer-starts on day one.

Compare to a school 3D printer: design, print, test, reprint without posting the part to Taiwan. Same idea, billion-dollar tools. ~5 min.

Two Chip Families

Earth — edge inference

  • Tesla AI5 and AI6
  • Optimus, Cybercab, FSD
  • Watts per inference, on the vehicle
  • About 20% of planned output
  • Repair shops and OTA updates exist

Space — orbital compute

  • SpaceX D3, radiation-hardened
  • Starlink-class craft and orbital data centres
  • Survive cosmic rays and vacuum thermal swing
  • About 80% of planned output
  • You cannot send a technician to LEO on Tuesday
“Inference on the robot, training in orbit” is the story. 80/20 split surprises people who think this is only a car chip plant. ~4 min.

Case study

Seaholm to Gibbons Creek

Austin · 21 March 2026 Intel joins · April 2026 Grimes County · summer 2026

Elon Musk announced Terafab at the retired Seaholm Power Plant in Austin — a former power station, which is a pointed backdrop for a project that will need gigawatts. Intel joined weeks later. Grimes County then approved a tax-abatement package around the old Gibbons Creek steam-plant site. Tesla and SpaceX later confirmed an initial multi-billion-dollar cheque and a 100-million-square-foot plan.

Classroom parallel

This is an emerging-technology case: a named place, named firms, a public hearing, and neighbours who worry about water, traffic and housing. Assessment work should cite who / where / when, not “a big US factory”.

Seaholm is a real building they can search. Keep politics light; keep the evidence trail. ~6 min.

Common Misreads

SymptomLikely mix-upFix
“Tera means terabyte” Confusing storage with power / compute Tera = trillion. The target is ~1 TW of AI compute per year, not a hard drive
“They will replace TSMC next month” Ignoring process learning and yield Prototype first (thousands of wafers). Full-scale is a years-long ramp with Intel in the loop
“It’s only a Tesla car-chip plant” Missing the 80% space split Edge chips for robots and cars; majority capacity for orbital compute
“The synchrotron is a physics toy” Thinking of CERN, not lithography An electron synchrotron can feed the patterning tools that print nanometre features
“Just more warehouses in a field” Ignoring water, power and cleanrooms Fabs need ultra-pure water, stable power, and Class-clean air — that is why the reservoir matters
“The night picture is a photograph” Treating a concept overlay as as-built Label it as a plan. The August 2026 graphic shows clearing, not a finished roof

Jargon Buster

Fab
A semiconductor fabrication plant — the factory that prints chips onto silicon wafers.
Foundry
A company that manufactures chips designed by others. TSMC is the classic example.
Wafer
A round disc of ultra-pure silicon. Dozens or hundreds of chips are printed on each one, then cut apart.
Lithography
Printing the chip’s pattern onto the wafer with light (or electrons). The mask is the stencil.
Electron synchrotron
A ring that accelerates electrons. Here it is drawn as a lithography / research tool on the factory spine, not a tourist collider.
Process node
A shorthand for how small the features are (for example 2 nm or Intel 14A). Smaller is harder and usually more efficient.
HBM / memory
High-bandwidth memory stacked near the processor. AI chips starve without it.
Advanced packaging
Putting several chips (logic, memory) into one module with tiny interconnects — not a cardboard box.
Yield
The fraction of chips on a wafer that actually work. New processes start with painful yield.
Inference
Running a trained model to make a decision — “what is that pedestrian doing?” — often on the device.
Training
The power-hungry stage that teaches the model. Terafab’s space story is partly about putting that load in orbit.
Radiation-hardened
Designed to survive cosmic rays. Ordinary phone chips glitch in orbit.
AI5 / AI6
Tesla’s fifth- and sixth-generation in-house AI chips for vehicles and humanoid robots.
D3
SpaceX’s high-power, space-qualified chip family called out for Terafab’s orbital line.
Vertical integration
Owning several stages of the supply chain. Here: design through test in one building.
Terawatt (TW)
A trillion watts. Terafab’s name is a compute-capacity boast, not a storage unit.
Let them search yield, lithography, inference. Sixteen terms — enough for the assignment glossary. ~5 min.

Key Takeaways

A fab is a factory

AI products are limited by wafers, water and power — not only by clever prompts.

One-roof loop

Design → mask → wafer → package → test, then change the mask. That is the prototype’s reason to exist.

Mostly space

Edge chips for robots and cars are the minority. Orbital compute is the volume story.

Plans are not photos

The night render is a concept. August 2026 shows clearing beside a reservoir. Caption your evidence.

Four pillars, then stop. ~3 min.
