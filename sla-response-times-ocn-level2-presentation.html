SLA Response Times

How service levels shape support priorities, clocks and trust.

OCN NI Level 2-3 · IT support / service delivery

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, you will be able to:

  • Define an SLA in support-desk language as an agreed, measurable service target.
  • Distinguish response time from resolution time with clear examples.
  • Prioritise tickets using impact × urgency into P1–P4 (Critical–Low).
  • Simulate an SLA clock and recognise on-track, warning and breach states.
  • Explain why unrealistic targets destroy trust and flood real outages with noise.

Why this matters

When everything is “urgent”, nothing is

Helpdesks that treat every ticket as P1 burn the Critical clock on password resets while a real campus outage waits. Published SLAs and a priority matrix protect the people who are actually blocked.

Real consequence: Trust collapses when users see “we’ll respond in 15 minutes” but the queue is full of low-impact noise.

Core concept: what is an SLA?

SLA ideas

  • A promise to the customer or business
  • Measurable targets (response, resolve, uptime)
  • Reported and reviewed, not just printed
  • Drives who gets helped first

In support language

  • “We will acknowledge a Critical ticket within 15 minutes.”
  • “We will aim to restore service within 4 hours.”
  • Targets must be realistic for staffing and hours
  • Business hours vs 24×7 clocks matter
Remember: An SLA is not a wish list. It is an agreed target you can measure and defend.
Timing: 5 minutes. Ask learners to invent one bad SLA (“fix every email in 30 seconds”) and why it fails.

Response time vs resolution time

Response time

  • First meaningful contact
  • Acknowledge, triage, start work
  • Clock often starts at ticket open
  • Does not mean the issue is fixed

Resolution time

  • Time to restore service or close
  • May need parts, vendors or change windows
  • Often longer than response target
  • Users care about both — be clear which you quote
RespondEngage & triage
ResolveFix & close
BothTracked in ITSM
Timing: 5 minutes. Role-play: agent replies “looking at it” in 10 minutes but fix takes 3 hours — response met, resolve still open.

Priorities: P1–P4 (Critical–Low)

P1 Critical

  • Whole site / campus offline
  • Major exam system down
  • Fastest response clock

P2 High

  • Department or class blocked
  • Important service degraded
  • Same-day push

P3 Medium

  • One user heavily impacted
  • Workaround may exist
  • Standard queue

P4 Low

  • How-to / request
  • Cosmetic or non-blocking
  • Longer target OK
Timing: 4 minutes. Map each priority to a college example before revealing the matrix slide.

Breach risk: when clocks run past target

Warning

Approaching the target — escalate, re-prioritise, or warn the customer.

Breach

Target missed. Record why, notify stakeholders, and recover the service.

Reporting

Breach rates feed service reviews. Patterns show staffing or process gaps.

Trust

Honest targets beat fantasy SLAs that always breach and train users to ignore them.

Timing: 5 minutes. Stress that a breach is a process event, not personal failure — but ignoring it is a failure.

Case study: college helpdesk flood

Mon 09:05Campus Wi‑Fi down
40+“Urgent” resets
P1Clock breached

What happened

  • All tickets marked urgent by default
  • Password-reset noise filled the queue
  • Real outage sat behind low-impact work

Impact

  • P1 response SLA breached
  • Teaching spaces stayed offline longer
  • Users lost trust in the desk

Fix

  • Publish priority matrix + SLAs
  • Train agents to reclassify noise
  • Major incident channel for outages

Jargon buster

SLAService Level Agreement: an agreed, measurable promise about service performance such as response time.
Response timeHow quickly support makes first meaningful contact after a ticket is logged.
Resolution timeHow long it takes to restore service or fully close the issue.
PriorityThe ordered importance of a ticket, often P1 Critical through P4 Low.
ImpactHow widely or severely the issue affects users or the organisation.
UrgencyHow quickly the issue needs attention based on time pressure.
BreachWhen the clock runs past the agreed SLA target without meeting it.
TriageQuickly assessing and classifying tickets so the right work is done first.
ITSMIT Service Management: practices and tools for delivering and supporting IT services.
Service deskThe front-line team that logs, prioritises and handles support requests.
Major incidentA high-impact outage managed with a dedicated process, often at P1.
EscalationMoving an issue to a higher skill level or manager when needed.
OLAOperational Level Agreement: internal targets between teams that support the SLA.
Business hoursThe working window when SLA clocks usually run for non-24×7 services.

Key takeaways

SLAs set expectations

Agreed, measurable promises — not slogans.

Respond ≠ resolve

First contact and full fix are different clocks.

Impact × urgency

Priority matrices beat “everything urgent”.

Watch the breach

Warning states and honest targets protect trust.
