How They Are Trained

Nobody types walk_nicely() for every joint. The G1 learns a policy — a mapping from “what I sense” to “what I do with my motors.”

Teleoperation

A human drives it with a controller or motion capture. Good for a first demo and for collecting examples.

Imitation

Copy a human demonstration. Faster than writing every joint angle by hand.

Reinforcement learning

The simulated robot tries, falls, tries again. A reward says “stay up and move forward.” Thousands of years of practice in a few hours of GPU time.

Sim-to-real

The policy is transferred onto the physical G1. Domain randomisation (friction, mass, lighting) stops it from only working in the fake world.

Level 2: reward = score in a game. Do not derive PPO. EDU SDK (Python / ROS 2) is how SRC students will eventually talk to it. ~6 min.

Practical: Name the Two Robots

Brief

SRC wants students to choose a name for each G1 EDU. You are naming college equipment that will appear in logs, demos and risk assessments.

  • Two different names — never “Robot 1 and Robot 1”
  • Easy to shout across the Urban Square hall
  • College-appropriate (no insults, no copyright traps you cannot defend)
  • One sentence of why each name fits the unit you picked
  • A safety line: who spots, where it may walk

Jargon Buster

G1 EDU
Unitree’s compact education humanoid (~132 cm). EDU adds SDK access, extra joints, payload and an on-board AI module.
H1
Unitree’s taller adult-size humanoid (~180 cm). SRC’s photos are G1-scale, not H1.
Degree of freedom (DoF)
One independent joint motion. More DoF means more poses — and more things to control.
LiDAR
A spinning or solid-state laser scanner that builds a 3D point cloud of the room so the robot knows where walls are.
Actuator
The motor-plus-gearbox at a joint. EDU knees can push about 120 N·m — strong, still not a forklift.
Payload
How much mass an arm can hold usefully. About 3 kg on G1 EDU — a tool or a bottle, not a person.
Policy
The trained “if I sense this, move like that” function that comes out of reinforcement learning.
Reinforcement learning
Trial and error with a score. Stay upright and move forward = points. Fall = no points.
Imitation learning
Copy a human demonstration instead of discovering the skill from scratch.
Teleoperation
A person drives the robot live. Useful for demos and for recording data.
Sim-to-real
Train in a physics simulator, then run the same policy on the metal robot. Randomising the sim helps it survive the real floor.
SDK / ROS 2
The EDU software kit. Students can write Python or C++ and talk to the robot; ROS 2 is a common robotics middleware.
Jetson Orin
NVIDIA’s on-board AI computer on EDU — vision and policy inference without a desktop GPU strapped to its back.
E-stop
Emergency stop. First thing you locate before a demo. Cuts motion when a person or a chair is in the way.
Bipedal
Walks on two legs. Great in buildings made for people. Easy to fall compared with four wheels or four legs.
Dexterous hand
Optional multi-finger gripper. Lets EDU pinch and hold, not just bump with a stump.
Let them search sim-to-real, payload, EDU. Sixteen terms for the assignment glossary. ~5 min.
